# EXTANT research and content audit

Reviewed 22 September 2026. This separates fresh evidence from the earlier site's source notes. It is not a claim that every historical detail has been independently confirmed.

## Lineup and naming

The site's previously transcribed video credits identify Novem Htoo (vocals), PZ (bass), Min Khant (guitar), and “Japan Idiots” (drums) on P Tripper, Main Character Syndrome and Dog Eat Dog. The client requested the drummer's public profile as **Oo Japan**, from **Idiots**. The member profile and current lineup use that name; the historical video credit transcription retains “Japan Idiots”. This updates the existing drummer rather than adding a fifth member.

Japan's association with the Myanmar band Idiots is corroborated by the [MusicBrainz credit listing for လူအဂီတ](https://musicbrainz.org/release/3940779c-9326-4cdb-9dc8-bc0cde844a71), which identifies Japan on drums and percussion, and by the [Last.fm album biography](https://www.last.fm/music/Idiots/Lu%2BAh%2BGita). These are community-maintained catalogues, not official membership announcements. They disagree on the album date (2009 versus 2010); the site does not repeat that date. “Oo Japan” is the client's requested spelling, rather than a legal name verified by these catalogues. No date of joining EXTANT is established.

There are unrelated artists named Extant and Idiots. Search results for the Seoul punk band at idiots.band do not concern this musician. Broad streaming artist pages may merge catalogues; do not import every release they display.

## Novem Htoo before and alongside EXTANT

A contemporary [Myanmar Times article, 14–20 January 2013, page 35](https://uzo.sakura.ne.jp/burma/mmt/mmt_data/mmt_2013/mmt_01_2013/mmt_661-03_01_2013.pdf) identifies Novem Htoo as Nightmare's vocalist. It describes the band's self-produced album work and the difficulty of funding underground metal in Myanmar. This supports adding Nightmare to his biography. It also makes the old site's broad “metalist since 2010” phrasing too simplistic; the updated bio avoids asserting a start year.

[The Faces of Rock exhibition producer's account](https://www.elodiesobczak.com/the-faces-of-rock) names “Novem Htoo (Extant)” among the musicians photographed for the Yangon exhibition held 6 April–19 May 2019. Raymond and Ringo of Idiots also appear in the project. This provides contemporary evidence of Novem's EXTANT association in 2019 and situates both bands in the wider Yangon rock community. The site's images were not copied from this project; photographic rights remain with the photographer.

[The Irrawaddy, 17 June 2019](https://burma.irrawaddy.com/lifestyle/entertainment/2019/06/17/195017.html) reports his victory in The Voice Myanmar Season 2 and says he sang an Idiots song in the final. [Kerrang's October 2019 coverage](https://www.kerrang.com/watch-this-contestant-on-the-voice-myanmar-absolutely-nail-slipknots-spit-it-out) independently describes his Slipknot “Spit It Out” blind audition. The publication month of the later international coverage should not be mistaken for the audition date.

The [January 2024 Jimmy Ko Ko podcast listing](https://podcasts.apple.com/ca/podcast/jimmy-ko-ko-podcast/id1656343121) identifies him as EXTANT's vocalist and The Voice Myanmar Season 2 winner. The episode itself was not transcribed during this review, so no additional biographical claims are attributed to it.

## Discography and collaborations

[Apple Music's Revolution Means release page](https://music.apple.com/us/album/revolution-means-single/1703689636) gives 31 August 2023, one track, and Novem Htoo as the artist. This belongs to his solo work, not automatically to EXTANT's album discography. The page alone does not verify every featured musician or production credit from the old site.

[Apple Music's Body Bags listing](https://music.apple.com/us/album/body-bags-single/1535026695) names Kyar Pauk, Raymond, Han Nay Tar and Novem Htoo, dated 10 October 2020. It is further evidence of cross-band collaboration, and should not be relabelled an EXTANT single. Apple's Revolution Means page also links to I, Destroyer and Rise Up; their full credits have not been audited here.

The existing 11-entry video archive is retained with its recorded dates and view-count snapshot. The official [EXTANT channel](https://www.youtube.com/@extantband3863) and [Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/Extantband/) were throttled during this review. The prior site's detailed 2025–2026 video credits, six-song concept-album announcement, HAIYAR narrative, founding year, and production claims therefore remain inherited claims, not newly verified findings. Do not invent an album release date or a complete current discography from these gaps.

- Corrected the drummer's displayed name and Idiots association; preserved the distinction from historical credit wording.
- Added the supported Nightmare background to Novem's profile and the final's Idiots connection to the history text.
- Replaced the stale “Ten years” heading with “Since 2015”.
- Derived archive filter totals and video-play total from the actual release array; derived years from the configured founding year. Followers remain a manually maintained snapshot.
- Added content-managed portraits and a photo archive without assigning an unidentified photo to Oo Japan.
- Preserved the existing design, local video assets, custom domain, and primary navigation.

Before treating the portfolio as the band's authoritative press kit, the band should confirm: the preferred member names and roles; whether each is a permanent or session member; permissioned individual portraits; exact album title and release status; preferred booking contact; and whether old follower/view counts should remain visible. These checks do not prevent the requested content-management workflow from functioning.
